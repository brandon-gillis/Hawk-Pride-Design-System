# Customer Center of Gravity

## Strategic Decision

The brand should center on the **regional recreational off-road enthusiast who wants a trip-worthy adventure, but is not necessarily an extreme rock crawler**.

## Primary Customer

A regional recreational off-road enthusiast who:

- owns a Jeep, 4x4, SxS, buggy, or similar off-road vehicle
- treats off-roading as a meaningful hobby
- is willing to travel for a worthwhile trip
- commonly visits with friends, family, or a club
- enjoys exploration, scenery, fun, and challenge in different proportions depending on the trip
- wants legitimate off-road terrain without every visit needing to be extreme
- values a complete weekend experience rather than simple trail access
- may camp, bring an RV, or stay in a cabin
- wants confidence that the chosen experience fits the vehicle, experience level, group, and available time

The central customer is not defined by one vehicle category.

The common denominator is that off-roading is something they actively do and they want the trip to feel like an **adventure**, not merely access to some trails.

## Core Job to Be Done

> **Give me a weekend off-road adventure that feels worth the trip, fits my group and my machine, and gives us something memorable to experience together.**

Accomplishment can be an important part of that experience, but it is not the only reason for the trip.

## Customer Experience North Star

The intended post-visit reaction is:

> **"This was an amazing trip. I saw so many things and accomplished something. This place is beautiful and fun. I can't wait to do the next adventure."**

This captures the complete desired experience better than accomplishment alone.

## Typical Decision Context

The customer may be deciding on Thursday or Friday what to do that weekend.

They may be coordinating with:

- a spouse or partner
- children
- several friends
- another family
- a riding club

Different people in the group may have different vehicles, skill levels, and comfort levels.

Typical questions include:

- What can we ride?
- Is there enough here to justify the drive?
- What will we get to see?
- Can my rig handle these trails?
- Is there something suitable for everyone in the group?
- Where can we stay?
- How much will the weekend cost?
- What is the one thing we should not miss?
- What should we try this time?
- What could we come back and do next time?

## Customer Needs

### Before the visit: confidence and anticipation

They need to understand:

- what Hawk Pride offers
- what different adventures are available
- what their vehicle can handle
- trail difficulty
- what different members of the group can do
- what they will see and experience
- lodging and camping options
- prices
- event conditions
- arrival and check-in expectations

The planning experience should make the trip feel exciting before they arrive, not merely reduce uncertainty.

### At the park: exploration, choice, and direction

They need to know:

- what to do today
- where to go
- what is worth seeing
- how hard a route really is
- what fits the time available
- what the memorable experiences are
- how to navigate without unnecessary uncertainty

### During the experience: beauty, fun, and meaningful challenge

The experience should give customers:

- places worth seeing
- enjoyable riding
- shared moments with their group
- opportunities to take on something meaningful
- enough variation that the day feels like an adventure rather than a sequence of trails

### Around the experience: belonging

The park's friendly, informal, social culture is part of the product.

Important elements include:

- groups
- campfires
- shared stories
- other machines
- clubs
- events
- regulars
- family participation

### After the visit: memory, accomplishment, and anticipation

The customer should leave with:

- the feeling that the trip itself was worth taking
- memories of places they saw and things they did
- something they accomplished
- photos, stories, or artifacts that help preserve the experience
- a clear sense that there is another adventure waiting next time

## Supporting Audiences

### Families and Mixed-Skill Groups

Role: major secondary audience and growth use case.

Strategic rule:

**Family-friendly should be a capability of Hawk Pride, not the identity of the brand.**

Families already participate at the park. Designed easy and moderate experiences can make the park substantially more accessible without weakening its off-road credibility.

### Hardcore Crawlers and Rock Bouncers

Role: credibility audience.

They matter because they provide:

- authenticity
- signature terrain
- spectacle
- major-event energy
- word-of-mouth credibility
- proof that Hawk Pride is not manufacturing an artificial version of adventure

Strategic rule:

**Hardcore riders should give the brand teeth without defining the entire customer experience.**

### Jeep / 4x4 Enthusiasts

Role: major secondary audience.

Strong fit with:

- exploration
- progression
- named trails
- curated adventures
- trail accomplishment
- badges and collectibles
- social proof
- repeat visits

### SxS Riders

Role: major secondary audience.

Strong fit with:

- group riding
- multi-day visits
- camping and RV use
- scenic exploration
- curated trail adventures
- moderate-to-advanced terrain

Strategic rule:

Do not narrow Hawk Pride into a "SxS park."

### Clubs and Organized Groups

Role: acquisition and repeat-visit channel.

Groups aggregate the primary customer rather than representing a separate brand identity.

### Major-Event Attendees

Role: commercially important event customer.

Events can draw hundreds or thousands of visitors and should remain a major part of the business, but the episodic event audience should not define the ordinary-weekend brand.

### Casual / Local Riders

Role: peripheral but welcome audience.

Hawk Pride should serve them, but should not optimize the brand around low-cost local trail access.

## Customer Center vs. Customer Range

The center of gravity does not mean Hawk Pride only serves one customer type.

It means the park should be designed first for the customer whose desires best align with the business Hawk Pride is trying to build.

The core customer sits between two useful edges:

- families and newer riders create accessibility and growth
- hardcore crawlers create authenticity and credibility

The recreational enthusiast in the middle benefits from both while also valuing the scenic, social, fun, and exploratory dimensions of the park.
