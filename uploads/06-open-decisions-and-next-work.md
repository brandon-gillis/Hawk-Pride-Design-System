# Open Decisions and Next Work

## Current Position

Competitive positioning and customer center of gravity are established as working strategy.

The brand platform has undergone an important correction:

> **Adventure is the center of the brand. Challenge and accomplishment are important parts of the adventure, but they are not the entire brand.**

The previous challenge-led master promise has been superseded. The broader adventure-level core brand promise is now locked as **“A whole mountain of real off-road adventure, your way.”** The emotional payoff is also locked as **“A trip that feels genuinely worth taking—full of discovery, fun, shared experience, and accomplishment, with enough left unexplored to make you want another one.”** The positioning statement, mission, and six brand principles are also locked. The remaining platform should be completed from that foundation before visual identity begins.

A preservation guardrail is also established: **future Hawk Pride should reveal and organize what people already love about the park rather than overwrite it.** The brand must remain rugged, real, friendly, free, scenic, social, varied, and fun even as planning, facilities, navigation, and product design improve.

## Experience North Star

The desired post-visit reaction is:

> **"This was an amazing trip. I saw so many things and accomplished something. This place is beautiful and fun. I can't wait to do the next adventure."**

This should be used to test future positioning, promise, mission, principles, and creative work.


## Visitor Experience Standards Now Established

Every Hawk Pride visitor should be able to count on:

1. **Real off-roading** — legitimate terrain and challenge remain central to the park's credibility.
2. **Friendly, down-to-earth people** — welcoming and helpful without corporate hospitality theater.
3. **A beautiful place to get lost in for a while** — scenery and exploration are part of the product.
4. **More to discover than you planned** — side quests, landmarks, obstacles, routes, and unexpected discoveries.
5. **Rugged, but cared for** — clean bathhouses, functioning lodging/camping, maintained trails, trustworthy maps/signs, without over-manicuring the park.
6. **Something worthwhile for everyone in the group** — different people can have different successful versions of the same trip.
7. **A hell of a lot of fun** — fun is a core outcome, not a byproduct of achievement.
8. **Freedom to make the weekend your own** — Hawk Pride creates options and confidence, not a required script.

### Preservation test

Future decisions should be tested against this question:

> **Does this make Hawk Pride easier to discover, enjoy, and return to without making it feel sanitized, corporate, over-programmed, or less free?**

## Important Questions Still Open

### How should the tagline be kept broad in practice?

The master tagline is now **“Go conquer something.”** The word **“something”** is intentionally open. Future creative should show different kinds of accomplishment rather than allowing “conquer” to become shorthand for extreme difficulty alone.

The governing principle remains: **Hawk Pride does not define what conquering means. The customer does.**

### How should ruggedness and accessibility balance?

The brand must avoid two failure modes:

1. becoming so hardcore that newer or mixed-skill customers assume the park is not for them
2. becoming so family-friendly and polished that experienced riders perceive it as softened or inauthentic

### How do we make the mountain tangible?

The brand should not rely on generic beauty or adventure claims. Product and content should reveal:

- climbs and descents
- alternate routes and lines
- destinations and overlooks where applicable
- signature terrain and obstacles
- creeks and changing environments across the mountain
- side quests and places customers can stumble into
- routes that feel like journeys rather than trail lists
- the unusual density of things to ride and discover in one mountain

### Which reasons to believe are current versus future?

Current strengths must be separated from capabilities still being developed.

For example:

Current:

- terrain
- extreme credibility
- mountain terrain and riding density
- events
- community
- cabins / RV / camping

Future or incomplete:

- full trail-navigation system
- curated adventure portfolio
- consistent difficulty standards
- completion rewards
- complete visit-planning system

The brand can point toward the future, but must not promise an experience that operations cannot yet deliver.
