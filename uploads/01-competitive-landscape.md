# Competitive Landscape

## Purpose

Map the real set of alternatives that shape a customer's decision about whether Hawk Pride is worth the weekend.

The competitive set is broader than other off-road parks. Hawk Pride competes against:

- nearby private off-road parks
- larger destination parks
- off-road resorts
- public trail systems
- club rides and private-property weekends
- local riding options
- choosing not to make a destination trip at all

## Core Benchmark Set

### Hawk Pride Mountain Offroad

Current physical strengths:

- 1,000+ acres
- 120+ rock-crawling trails
- terrain ranging from accessible four-wheel-drive riding through extreme rock crawling
- cabins, RV sites, camping, major events, signature obstacles, and a strong community culture

Strategic observation:

Hawk Pride already has a credible riding product. The principal weakness is that the product is not yet organized, explained, navigated, or sold as intentionally as the terrain deserves.

### Adventure Off Road Park

What it effectively owns:

- serious technical challenge
- clear progression from easier trails into hard rock crawling
- credibility with hardcore riders

Strategic implication:

Hawk Pride should not attempt to win by simply claiming to be the hardest or most extreme.

### Top Trails OHV Park

What it effectively owns:

- broad accessible riding
- meaningful scale
- family-oriented facilities and experiences
- beginner-to-expert usefulness

Strategic implication:

Top Trails is an important benchmark for how a park can make mixed-experience riding understandable and accessible.

### Stony Lonesome OHV Park

What it effectively owns:

- broad OHV utility
- public-park accessibility
- multiple vehicle types
- lodging and camping support

Strategic implication:

Hawk Pride should feel more distinctive, intentional, and emotionally memorable rather than competing on generic breadth.

### Windrock Park

What it effectively owns:

- scale
- 73,000 acres
- 300+ miles of trails
- year-round riding
- broad difficulty range
- extensive lodging, retail, mapping, rentals, and event infrastructure

Strategic implication:

Hawk Pride cannot and should not compete on acreage. Windrock demonstrates, however, how much planning infrastructure matters when selling a destination.

### Ride Royal Blue Resort

What it effectively owns:

- the off-road resort vacation
- ride-in/ride-out lodging
- extensive trail access
- rentals, fuel, store, guides, wash facilities, and other amenities

Strategic implication:

Hawk Pride should not attempt to become a resort copy. Royal Blue is useful as a benchmark for designing the entire trip rather than only the trail ride.

### Wooly's Off Road

What it effectively owns:

- inexpensive
- local
- uncomplicated riding

Strategic implication:

Hawk Pride should not compete primarily on being the cheapest place to ride.

### Wheelin' in the Country

What it effectively owns:

- local club-style riding
- community-oriented use
- mixed difficulty
- primitive camping and events

Strategic implication:

This is direct competition for riders who simply want somewhere nearby to spend a Saturday. Hawk Pride needs a stronger reason to justify travel.

## Substitute Competition

Important substitutes include:

- public OHV systems such as Turkey Bay
- forest roads and informal riding areas
- private land
- club weekends
- staying close to home

These alternatives matter because the customer is not always choosing between Hawk Pride and another commercial park. Often the real question is whether Hawk Pride is worth loading the machine, towing several hours, and committing the weekend.

## Attributes That Matter in the Competitive Decision

The useful comparison dimensions are:

- terrain and challenge
- difficulty variety
- vehicle compatibility
- events
- lodging and camping
- family and mixed-skill accessibility
- ease of planning
- trail navigation
- community and social atmosphere
- facilities
- price
- travel-worthiness
- memorable or signature experiences

## Main Competitive Learning

Challenge alone is not the whole product.

Customers also need confidence that:

- the terrain is worth traveling for
- their vehicle can handle what they choose
- the group can find suitable experiences
- they can plan the trip without excessive friction
- staying overnight will work
- they will not spend the weekend lost or uncertain
- there is something memorable to accomplish
