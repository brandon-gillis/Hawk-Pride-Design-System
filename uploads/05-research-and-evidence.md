# Research and Evidence

## Purpose

Preserve the evidence behind the strategic decisions so future work can distinguish established facts, directional evidence, field observations, inference, and open questions.

This document should not be treated as customer-facing copy.

## Internal Customer Data

### WaiverForever Dataset

Two WaiverForever CSV exports were reviewed from January 2024 through 2025-era records available in the current Drive folder.

Combined dataset:

- 1,210 waiver rows
- approximately 1,057 distinct normalized names
- 956 rows with a state that could be reasonably determined

### Geographic Distribution

Among records with a determinable state:

- Tennessee: 34.4%
- Alabama: 26.0%
- Mississippi: 17.3%
- Georgia: 8.6%

Tennessee + Alabama + Mississippi represented approximately **77.7%** of records with a known state.

Interpretation:

Hawk Pride has a strong regional Mid-South customer base rather than functioning only as a local Northwest Alabama park.

### Age

The median adult age was approximately:

- 42 in one waiver format
- 44 in the other waiver format

Interpretation:

The existing audience is not primarily very young recreational users. The center of gravity appears to be established adult enthusiasts, often in the 30s through 50s.

### Family Participation

Across the two waiver formats, more than 200 records explicitly listed participating minors or children associated with the signing adult.

This is a conservative signal because:

- not every family record necessarily lists children the same way
- only a parent/guardian row may list the minors
- data fields changed between waiver formats

Interpretation:

Family participation is already meaningfully present and is not merely an aspirational future audience.

### Driver / Passenger Behavior

In the newer waiver format:

- 359 records identified as driver
- 183 as passenger
- 23 as both driver and passenger

Interpretation:

A substantial portion of park participants are not simply individual vehicle operators. Group and shared-trip dynamics matter.

### Referral Source

In the older waiver form, "Friends" dominated the "Where did you hear about us?" field, with Facebook a distant secondary source.

Interpretation:

Word of mouth, social groups, and community relationships appear important to customer acquisition.

## Dataset Limitations

The waiver exports are directional evidence, not a clean customer census.

Limitations include:

- repeated customers may appear multiple times
- names may be entered inconsistently
- address fields are incomplete in some records
- waiver form structure changed over time
- minors may be captured under an adult record
- event and ordinary-weekend customers are mixed
- signing a waiver does not directly reveal customer spend
- the dataset does not reliably identify vehicle ownership or exact ride type

Therefore, the data supports strategic direction but should not be treated as a precise market-share or customer-demographic study.

## Field Observations

### SRRS 2026

Observed or documented:

- 488 numbered paper waivers were counted
- actual attendance was higher because waivers could cover multiple people
- working attendance estimate was more than 500 and possibly around 600
- many attendees had traveled meaningful distances
- RVs, tow rigs, trailers, and expensive off-road vehicles were common
- approximately 40 RVs appeared onsite
- cabins and serviced RV sites were full

- families and children were visibly part of the audience
- the atmosphere was highly social, friendly, informal, and community-driven

Interpretation:

Hawk Pride already demonstrates destination demand, equipment investment, overnight behavior, family participation, and strong social/community value.

## Product Observations

The park effectively contains two different riding environments:

1. rock, hill-climb, extreme, and major-event terrain
2. wooded terrain ranging from accessible to difficult, with scenic and family potential

The second product is substantially underdeveloped in the customer experience.

Current gaps include:

- incomplete trail marking
- difficulty uncertainty
- limited curated routes
- limited arrival guidance
- limited route recommendations by vehicle or skill
- weak connection between terrain and purchasable adventure products

Interpretation:

The park already possesses more customer range than its current presentation communicates.

## Research on Hawk Pride's Existing Identity

A focused review was conducted specifically to avoid building a future brand that erases why people already come to Hawk Pride today.

### Existing public positioning

Historical and current descriptions of Hawk Pride emphasize a broad range of legitimate off-road terrain rather than a narrow single-use product. Public language has included the idea of **"mild to wild"** and riding for everything from relatively stock 4x4s through serious rock-crawling vehicles.

Interpretation:

The future brand should preserve the park's authentic off-road credibility and broad difficulty range. Accessibility should be created through better information and product design, not by softening the terrain.

### Rider and visitor commentary

Across enthusiast discussions and visitor-review sources, recurring themes include:

- terrain variety and legitimate rock-crawling challenge
- the ability to enjoy the park in vehicles that are not extreme builds
- friendly people / staff and a strong informal community feeling
- the experience of riding a mountain with climbs, drops, creeks, wooded sections, obstacles, and places that feel removed from normal life
- cabins and camping as useful parts of the experience
- riding with families, friends, children, passengers, and different vehicle types
- simple enjoyment: people describe the park as fun and worth returning to

Interpretation:

The existing identity is not only "hardcore rock crawling." It is a combination of **real off-roading, freedom, variety, mountain terrain, friendly community, and fun**.

### Strategic implication

The future Hawk Pride should not behave like a scripted attraction where the park dictates exactly how the customer must experience it.

The product strategy can add:

- clearer maps
- named adventures
- difficulty guidance
- cleaner and more dependable facilities
- better trip planning
- points of interest
- optional challenges and completion systems

while still preserving the customer's freedom to wander, explore, improvise, help friends, camp, and create their own version of the weekend.

The resulting preservation principle is:

> **Reveal and organize the existing magic. Do not overwrite it.**

## Competitive Research Conclusion

The meaningful strategic gap is not simply more difficult terrain, more acreage, or lower price.

The opportunity is to combine:

- authentic technical credibility
- broad terrain and experience variety
- mountain exploration and discovery
- easier planning
- clearer choice
- fun and social group use
- meaningful challenge and accomplishment
- a complete weekend experience
- enough depth to make another trip feel different

into a proposition that makes Hawk Pride worth the trip.

## Brand Strategy Interpretation Note

The research originally led strongly toward challenge, accomplishment, and "conquer" territory. Subsequent brand-platform work clarified that this interpretation was too narrow at the master-brand level.

The current strategic hierarchy is:

1. adventure as the complete experience
2. exploration and discovery
3. the mountain / place itself and fun
4. challenge and accomplishment
5. anticipation of another adventure

The evidence supporting challenge and accomplishment remains valid; those ideas have been **repositioned within the brand**, not discarded.

A later identity-preservation research pass added another constraint: future brand and experience design must preserve Hawk Pride's existing rough-edged authenticity, friendly community, freedom, terrain variety, scenery, and fun. The strategy is additive and clarifying, not replacement-oriented.

## Brand Insight: The Mountain and Off-Road Density

A later strategy pass surfaced a more distinctive interpretation of Hawk Pride's physical product. Generic language about "beautiful woods" or "scenery" is too weak and too interchangeable with other outdoor brands.

The stronger truth is that **Hawk Pride is on a mountain**, and a large amount of off-road activity is concentrated there. Riders can climb, descend, cross, loop, change difficulty, find another trail, hit an obstacle, stop for a side quest, and keep moving without the park needing the acreage of the largest destinations.

Working interpretation:

- the mountain is a brand asset, not background scenery
- compactness can be reframed as **density** rather than a limitation
- verticality, alternate lines, route changes, and side quests make the park feel busy with possibility
- the customer can do a lot in one trip while still leaving plenty of the mountain for next time

This insight directly informed the brand-attitude line:

> **A whole mountain of bad ideas.**

Subsequent platform work retained this line for merchandise and campaign use rather than selecting it as the master tagline.

## Verbal Identity Insight

Earlier promise language became too generic when it leaned on words such as "beautiful," "explore," and "adventure" without enough Hawk Pride attitude.

The emerging verbal voice is therefore:

- direct rather than polished
- playful rather than inspirational
- confident rather than grandiose
- informal rather than corporate
- slightly mischievous rather than sanitized

The completed language system now assigns distinct roles:

- **A whole mountain of real off-road adventure, your way.** — core brand promise
- **Go conquer something.** — master tagline
- **Every trip should feel like an adventure worth remembering.** — internal experience ambition
- **Ride what you came for. Find what you didn't.** — discovery expression
- **Mild. Wild. And a whole lot in between.** — heritage/range expression
- **A whole mountain of bad ideas.** — merchandise/campaign attitude line

These are a language system with different strategic jobs, not competing taglines.
